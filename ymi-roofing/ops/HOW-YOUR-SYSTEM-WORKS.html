How Your Website Works

A plain-English guide to what your new website does, how a customer reaches you, and the one thing you need to do to turn it into paying jobs. No jargon — if anything here isn't clear, ask and we'll fix the wording.

What you've got

A website at ymiroofing.com.au whose job is simple: turn a stranger who found you on their phone into a quote request sitting in front of you. Someone searches for a roofer, lands on your page, sees your work and your details, and either taps to call you or fills in a short form. That's it. Everything on the site is built to push toward that one moment — a real person asking you for a price.

What happens when someone enquires

  1. The customer fills in the quote form and taps Send.
  2. You get an email with their name, phone, suburb and the job — NEEDS SWITCHING ON (Aurora confirms this is live and sends you a test lead before launch).
  3. Optionally, you also get an instant text so you never miss one — NEEDS SWITCHING ON (built and ready; needs a Twilio account connected).
  4. The customer gets a short "we got your request" email back, so they know it landed.
  5. You call them back. This part is you — see below.
Before go-live: the form only sends if the email key is switched on in the hosting settings. Until Aurora confirms it and you've received a test lead, treat lead delivery as not yet proven. This is the single most important thing to tick off before you promote the site.

The one thing you need to do

Call every lead back fast — ideally within a few minutes. The tradie who calls back first almost always wins the job. A lead that sits for a day is usually a lost job, no matter how good the website is. The site's whole value is getting you that call early; the rest is up to you.
